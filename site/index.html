Frequently asked questions

Everything about capturing, annotating and sharing with RvgeShot.

Is RvgeShot free?

Yes. RvgeShot is completely free, with no account and no ads. Download it for Windows 10 or 11; a macOS build is in testing.

How do I take a screenshot with RvgeShot?

Press your Print Screen hotkey, drag to select any region of the screen, annotate it if you like, then copy, save locally, or share it via a private link.

Can RvgeShot record my screen?

Yes. Press the recording hotkey, drag out the area you want, and RvgeShot records it straight to MP4 (H.264) or GIF — with a live timer, pause, and the mouse cursor drawn in. Windows never shipped a proper built-in recorder; this is it.

Can I draw on a recording while it runs?

Yes — the same toolset you get on a screenshot. Arrows, shapes, pen, marker, numbered steps, text and blur / redact, drawn live over the area you’re recording and baked into the video. The toolbar itself never shows up in the recording.

Why does Windows warn me when I install it?

RvgeShot isn’t code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may show “Windows protected your PC”. Click More info → Run anyway. The installer is built automatically from the public source code on GitHub.

Does RvgeShot work on both Windows and Mac?

RvgeShot runs on Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit) today. The macOS build for Apple Silicon and Intel is in testing and goes up on the download page as soon as it’s ready.

What makes RvgeShot more private than other screenshot tools?

Everything stays on your computer: no account, no telemetry, and the app works fully offline, with blur / redact as a first-class tool. Private share links — ~128-bit entropy, optional password, expiry of 1 hour to never, never indexed by search engines — arrive in an upcoming update.
